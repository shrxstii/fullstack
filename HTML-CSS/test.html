Slow Mornings, Deep Woods

There's a particular quiet that settles over a forest just before the light breaks through — the kind that asks nothing of you. Every step on the damp trail feels like a small negotiation with stillness, a reminder that not every morning needs a plan.

The fog doesn't lift so much as it thins, pulling back from the trees one branch at a time until the path ahead simply appears. Somewhere behind the mist a bird starts its first call of the day, unbothered by how early it is, and for a moment the whole forest seems to hold its breath before beginning again.

By the time the sun clears the ridge, the trail is warm underfoot and the quiet has changed shape — no longer empty, but full, the way mornings are when you've actually shown up for them.
